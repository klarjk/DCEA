# Pitfalls and Limits

Things to check before giving advice. Violating an item in this document sends out a prescription that contradicts the author's theory.

---

## Forbidden Path

**When flavor is lacking, do not prescribe in the direction of "extracting more".** Not only raising the flow rate but also lengthening the brew time or grinding finer is the same path. The author tried this for years and confirmed it as a failure. (Source 01)

> "I have kept trying in the direction of raising the flow rate in order to bring out more of the diminished flavor, but the results were always bad."

The total flavor load is fixed at that point in time and cannot be increased by extraction. Forcing out flavor that is not there produces a negative, grainy result.

> **Do:** "the aroma is weak" → lower the flow rate and raise TDS, turning the direction toward sweetness and texture
> **Don't:** "the aroma is weak" → raise the flow rate or extract more to try to increase the flavor

Reasoning from general coffee knowledge falls into this pitfall every time. Always check right before giving advice.

## Where It Conflicts With Conventional Wisdom

The user may have heard the opposite advice elsewhere. On a conflict, **follow the author's theory but state that there is a conflict.**

| Item | Conventional wisdom | The author |
|---|---|---|
| Astringency | a sign of over-extraction | **Extraction continues after the flavor has run out.** An excessive peak flow velocity is only one route — channeling, an excessive beverage weight, and high flavor with high TDS together get there too |
| Greenness | a sign of under-extraction | **over-extraction of flavor** |
| Bitter, astringent, harsh taste | over-extraction | mostly **excessive olfactory stimulus** (a degassing or temperature problem) |
| Fines | responsible for body and sweetness | **responsible for flavor**. Remove them and the aroma dies |
| Center-biased extraction | bad without exception | extraction yield drops but **consistency actually improves** |
| Even extraction | if it is perfectly even, raising extraction yield still tastes good | **perfect evenness is impossible**, and there is a separate range of concentration and extraction yield that feels good |
| berry · sweet · floral | good sensory notes | **a defect** when they appear unrelated to the coffee |

Be especially careful with astringency and greenness. Advising by conventional wisdom makes **the direction of the prescription exactly opposite.**

## Measurement Pitfalls

- **The Decent's on-machine flow-rate display**: its calculation coefficient is tuned to espresso resistance, so for brewing it **reads 30~50% lower than actual**. Always judge by **scale-based brew flow rate**. (Source 06)
- **F compounds do not register on a refractometer.** It is normal for the TDS to be normal while the taste is off. Do not judge "no problem" from TDS alone. (Source 08)
- **FCR has no absolute scale.** The author deliberately did not set one. Do not give the user an absolute value like "your FCR is 1.4". State only **which direction relative to the last brew**. (Source 03)

## Equipment Dependence

The author attached this caveat repeatedly in article after article. Do not carry the numbers over as is.

> "You have to take into account that my equipment is overall **a yield-focused setup**. A machine with a high water debit or a grinder that gives a low extraction yield may need to secure an appropriate headspace." (Source 09)

> "**The detailed numbers and content of the condition ranges can change depending on each person's equipment.**" (Source 14)

- The author's grinder (P64 + SSP cast) is in the **clarity-focused, high-yield** family. Users of conical burrs or hand grinders get much weaker flavor.
- **The brew-ratio limit at which astringency does not appear differs for every grinder.** The author's 1:15 is not anyone else's.
- Pump capacity (water debit) differs by machine, so the same setting gives a different flow velocity.

When citing a number, **state that it is based on the author's equipment**, and guide the user to find their own baseline.

## What the Author States They Do Not Know

If asked, answer that it is not known. Do not make it up.

- **The chemical identity of the flavor compounds** — "it's not as if I majored in anything like that". The F/C split is not chemistry but **an operational classification by flow-velocity responsiveness**
- **Why the dose governs flavor intensity** — even at the same brew ratio, a larger dose gives stronger flavor. Only two candidates are offered, with no conclusion
- **The quantitative threshold for astringency** — the author has stated the condition under which it arises ("when extraction continues after F has run out"), but it is still a [Hypothesis], and **at what point F is depleted, and the threshold for each brewer and grinder, are unknown.** 4mlps is a measured value on the author's equipment, not a general constant
- **Why the appropriate brew ratio differs by grinder**
- **Why a high TDS at a low flow rate is not harsh**
- **The theory of espresso basket geometry is at the theory stage with no measurements** — "since measuring flow rate is not easy, I will skip over it rather than deal with it" (Source 05)

## Unresolved Tensions Within the Author's Writing

Points where the prescription can diverge. Fix the context with a follow-up question to the user.

**① Preinfusion flow rate and time** — the preinfusion article says "a higher flow rate → resistance↑" (Source 11), the single-origin article says "a longer time → resistance↓" (Source 14). **The axes differ, so both hold.** Always treat flow rate and duration separately.

The measurements in Source 18 add a **third axis** here — **ramp rate**. Even though the author ramped pressure earlier (low pressure 12 s → 6 s), peak resistance instead came out lower. Ramping pressure gradually presses the unwetted zone less [Hypothesis]. That is, one cannot assert that "shortening preinfusion raises resistance". **Check each of the three axes — flow rate · duration · ramp rate.**

**② Sign reversal of temperature** — at a higher temperature, early on gas release gives resistance↑ · speed↓, and late on speed↑. Do not answer with a single sign. (Source 16)

**③ Resistance→speed reversal by equipment type** — on a constant-flow machine resistance↑ actually makes it faster, while gravity-fed drip gets slower. Confirm which equipment it is before answering. (Source 15)
