# Extraction Model (The Author's Theory)

Everything in this document is the theory of the blog author (klar201). Where it conflicts with general coffee knowledge, follow this document and tell the user about the conflict.

Confidence tags: `[Verified]` direct experiment / repeated confirmation / `[Observed]` empirical observation / `[Theory]` physically derived / `[Hypothesis]` marked by the author as an estimate or hypothesis.
Source citations: `(Source 05)` = `sources/blog/05_*.txt`.

---

## Two State Variables

**① Total flavor load** — the amount of light flavor compounds that bean currently holds. Extraction cannot change it. Roasting, grinder, dose, and degassing determine it.

**② F/C ratio (FCR)** — the ratio of the light flavor compounds (F) pulled out by extraction to the heavy compounds (C). Adjusted with extraction variables.

> "The degree of a coffee's flavor compounds is already fixed at that point in time, so rather than trying to change it: when there is much, extract much and bring the flavor out as far as possible; when there is little, extract little and instead extract around sweetness and good texture." [Verified] (Source 01)

---

## F/C Compound Dynamics

**F (light flavor compounds)** — compounds washed out by the flow of water. **An increasing function of flow velocity**. Its reservoir is finite, so it is extracted intensively early on and then depleted. **A refractometer does not pick it up** (volatile). [Theory+Verified] (Source 07, 08)

There is an order inside F as well. **sour · citrus are the lightest**, while **greenish · dried fruit · viscous are relatively heavy within F**, so they come out after the earlier ones have largely left. The "relatively heavy vegetal flavor" in Source 07 refers to this ordering inside F, and **across the whole spectrum greenness is clearly on the F side.** Prescribe on the F basis as well.

**C (heavy compounds)** — compounds that steep out as time passes. **An increasing function of time**, unrelated to flow velocity. Chocolate- and caramel-family sweetness, body, texture. [Theory] (Source 07)

Raise the flow rate and F increases in absolute amount (washing off by kinetic energy), while C decreases along with it because the time shortens. The cause of the C decrease is not flow velocity but **the shortened time**.

**Perceptual layering** — when several stimuli arrive at once, only the strongest one is mainly sensed and the rest are masked. [Theory] (Source 07)

### The Condition Under Which Astringency Arises

**The astringent compound is extracted when water keeps passing through after F has run out.** That is the condition; an excessive peak flow velocity is only one of several routes to it. [Hypothesis] (Source 01)

> "I think the astringent compound is probably extracted heavily when water keeps passing through even after the flavor compounds have been excessively extracted and run out." (Source 01)

Because the F reservoir is finite, "after depletion" can hold **for the whole bed or for one zone alone**. Four routes lead to the same condition. [Hypothesis] (Source 01)

| Route | How it becomes "after depletion" |
|---|---|
| Excessive peak flow velocity | F is washed out all at once over a short stretch, and the flow that follows passes through an emptied bed |
| Channeling | a lot of water funnels through a narrow zone at high flow velocity, so **that zone alone** is depleted first while water keeps passing through it |
| Excessive beverage weight | too much water is passed through relative to the total flavor load, so the whole back half becomes a post-depletion stretch |
| High flavor and high TDS together | if the flow rate pulls out plenty of F and the time and grind size also drive C up, extraction continues past the point where F has run out |

**What pulls the depletion point earlier is a tier-1 variable.** When the total flavor load is small (insufficient fines, an insufficient dose, excessive degassing), it runs out earlier on the very same recipe. Note it, but prescribe from tier 2.

**Separate arising from perceiving.** At a very high concentration, sensitivity rises and astringency is perceived more easily even when no more of it is produced. (Source 07)

This is why the prescription for astringency runs toward **extracting less**. The goal is to remove the post-depletion stretch, so **raising the flow rate, lengthening the time, and grinding finer all run the wrong way**. Route triage and prescriptions are in `diagnosis.md`.

### FCR assessment table (Source 03)

| | F/C > 1 | F/C ≒ 1 | F/C < 1 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Sensory notes | greenish, dried fruit, sour | the coffee's own flavor | chocolaty, floral sweet, bitter |
| Character | harsh | appropriate | empty, muted, dull |
| Average flow rate | high | medium | low |

**FCR is independent of TDS.** Of two coffees alike at TDS 1.25%, one can be harsh and the other feel empty. [Verified] (Source 03)

**FCR has no absolute scale.** The author deliberately did not set one — "because each extraction environment and recipe differs, and because a coffee's degree of flavor extraction also changes as time passes". Use it only as a **relative shift** within one and the same setup. (Source 03)

## The Two Variable Tiers

**Tier 1 (fundamental)** — roasting · grinder · dose · degassing. These change the total flavor load itself. Once one of them changes, "it is hardly possible to keep the same cup with recipe changes alone". **In diagnosis, only identify them; do not prescribe them.** (Source 02)

**Tier 2 (adjustable)** — temperature · flow rate · brew ratio · grind size. They affect TDS along with it. "the part a barista can actively intervene in". **Prescriptions go out only from here.** (Source 02)

### Separating the axes

- **Flow rate** → moves FCR. Judge by sensory evaluation.
- **Grind size** → sets TDS. "use grind size mainly to hit the target TDS, unrelated to flavor" (Source 02)
- **Temperature** → raises flavor and TDS together. It also increases flow rate slightly. (Source 06)

---

## Operating Principle

> "the broad direction of extracting fast at a high flow rate with low TDS when there is much flavor, and slowly at a low flow rate with high TDS when there is little flavor" [Verified] (Source 01)

The flow rate and the TDS target move **together in opposite directions**.

The higher the flow rate, the stronger the stimulus even at a low TDS. Target TDS on the author's equipment — acidity-forward (high flow rate) **1.2~1.25%** / sweetness-centered (low flow rate) **1.3~1.35%**. [Verified] (Source 06)

---

## Forbidden Path

**Do not raise the flow rate when flavor is lacking.** This is a path the author tried for years and confirmed as a failure.

> "I have kept trying in the direction of raising the flow rate in order to bring out more of the diminished flavor, but the results were always bad." (Source 01)

With a bean whose flavor has faded, **lower the flow rate and raise the TDS** and go toward sweetness and texture. Force the flow rate up and there is a high chance of a grainy, negative result. [Verified] (Source 01)

> **Do:** "the aroma is weak" → increase the number of pours to lower the flow rate and raise the target TDS
> **Don't:** "the aroma is weak" → raise the flow rate to pull out more flavor

## Proxy Indicators for Flow Rate

Diagnosis is possible even without a flowmeter.

- **Average flow rate = beverage weight ÷ brew time.** When the beverage weight is constant, **judging by brew time alone is fine**. (Source 03)
- **The flow rate in filter brewing is proportional to the water level inside the dripper.** The fewer the pours, the higher the flow rate; the more it is split, the lower. (Source 02)

The FCR axis works fully even without a refractometer. F compounds are not picked up by a refractometer to begin with, so sensory evaluation is the only means of judgment.

## Rejecting the Even Extraction Paradigm

The author does not accept the mainstream view that "with a perfectly even extraction, it tastes good even when you raise concentration and yield".

> "I think a perfectly even extraction cannot exist, and that given the structure of human perception there is a range of concentration and yield that comes across as a good impression." (Source 08)

Advise in the direction of **putting it inside the appropriate range**, not in the direction of raising the yield.

---

## The Definition of an Extraction Defect

**If different coffees taste the same, it is a defect.** A sensory note that appears from an excess of a particular flavor compound is "an extraction that makes everything take on the same taste at the same roast level regardless of the coffee, killing the coffee's character and fading the meaning of specialty". (Source 03)

A sensory note that sounds positive can also be a defect — berry, sweet, floral are all "things that appear only in a particular situation where the balance has collapsed". **floral** in particular is not the bean's own floral aroma as in Geisha, but "a pink impression that appears as the sweetness of chocolate and caramel scatters", and it **appears in every coffee** when you lower the flow rate, extract around sweetness, and raise the yield. (Source 03)

However, if it is present even in a balanced extraction, it is that coffee's character. Deliberately emphasizing a particular cup note is not a defect either.
