# Recipe Presets

**All of these are based on the author's equipment.** Do not prescribe them to the user as is. Use them as examples that explain structure and direction; the user finds their own values on their own equipment.

The author's equipment: grinder **Lagom P64 + SSP cast burr** (clarity-focused, tends to give high extraction yield), machine **Decent XL**, dripper **V60**.

---

## The Three-Stage Filter System (Source 01)

**Core structure**: move flow rate and target TDS **together in opposite directions**. When flavor is abundant, go faster and more dilute; when it is scarce, go slower and more concentrated.

| | Stage 1 | Stage 2 (reference) | Stage 3 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Dose | 15g | 18g | 21g |
| Temperature | 90 | 93 | 93 |
| Number of pours | 2 | 3 | 4 |
| Average flow rate | about 2ml/s | about 1.4~1.6ml/s | about 0.6~0.7ml/s |
| Target TDS | 1.3~1.35% | 1.4~1.45% | 1.5~1.55% |
| Filter | Hario 02 | Cafec light | Hario 01 |
| Kettle flow rate | 6ml/s | 6ml/s | **2ml/s** |

The dripper is V60 for all three stages.

### The author's diagnostic protocol

> "When I use the Stage 2 recipe for a first brew, I fix every other variable, **hit the target TDS of 1.4~1.45% with grind size alone, and then do a sensory evaluation**; if the strong flavor is negative I go to Stage 1, and conversely if the flavor is weak, flat and washed-out, with barley-tea graininess and a hollow in the middle, I use Stage 3."

```
        Start at Stage 2 · set TDS with grind size alone, then taste
                                      │
                ┌─────────────────────┴─────────────────────┐
   flavor too strong, negative                  flat · barley tea · empty
                ↓                                           ↓
             Stage 1                                     Stage 3
         flow rate↑ TDS↓                             flow rate↓ TDS↑
```

### Application by Stage

**Stage 1** — coffees with very strong flavor such as Nordic roasts and anaerobics. Based on the café Buff recipe.
```
60g (0:00, nested, center)
-240g (0:25, 120 center → 200 spiral → 240 center)
```
The high proportion of center pour forms flow quickly and brew time usually drops to **around 1:40**. When it comes out well, there are no harsh sensory notes such as greenish or dried fruit, and since TDS is 1.3~1.35% it is not thin or hollow in the middle either.
Alternative: **very low-dose flat-bottom recipes** like April or Glitch are fine too.

**Stage 2** — the default. A first attempt for unremarkable washed coffees and for naturals without strong fermentation. When a Stage 1 brew came out flat and washed-out, or when flavor has dropped over time while using Stage 1.
```
80g (0:00) - 185g (0:45) - 290g (1:30)
or
80g (0:00) - 175g (0:45) - 270g (1:30)
```
Filter: Cafec light has less sourness and is smoother than Hario 02. Brew time is slightly slower, within 10 seconds.

**Stage 3** — hand pour-over. 145g split into 4 pours over 21g → 100g beverage weight, then **add 100 ml dilution water**.
```
40g (0:00) - 80g (0:45) - 115g (1:15) - 145g (1:45) + 100ml water
```
45 s pre-wet, then 30~40 ml every 30 seconds. With an appropriate kettle flow rate it proceeds as **15 seconds pouring, 15 seconds waiting**.
Application: when the aroma has dropped while using Stage 2 and it has gone flat and washed-out. **When brewing with a hand grinder, start at this stage from the beginning.**

## Adjustment by Degassing Period (cited from La Cabra, Source 01)

The prototype of the author's three-stage system. As the days pass, **increase the number of pours and raise TDS** = lower the flow rate.

| Degassing | 0~2 weeks | 2~6 weeks | 6 weeks~ |
|---|---|---|---|
| Number of pours | 2 | 3 | 4 |
| Target TDS | 1.25~1.35% | 1.4~1.5% | 1.55~1.65% |
| Temperature | 90 | 94 | 94 |

The author sets temperature to 93 instead of 94, and TDS slightly lower than these. The current content of the original source may have changed.

## April 2 pour (Source 03)

April coffee roasters (Denmark, Patrik Rolf). The April brewer has an angle similar to Origami and a large bottom hole, so flow is fast.

| Dose | Water weight | Beverage weight | Kettle flow rate |
|---|---|---|---|
| 12~14g | 200g | 165~168ml (when the top of the bed has dried) | 10ml/s |

| Pour | Time | Water weight | Method |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 0:00~0:35 | 100g | 50g spiral → 50g center |
| 2 | 0:35~ | 100g | wash down the edges first → 50g spiral → 50g center |

The first pour of 100g is very large at **1:7.7**. Patrik Rolf does not see this as blooming, and aims at "wetting as fast and as evenly as possible, with the feeling of extracting from the very first pour".

**The author's interpretation**: pouring a large first pour at a high flow rate **secures bed resistance**, which prevents the astringency that appears easily at low doses.

**The author's modification** (for ordinary roast levels) — April's beans are Nordic style, so lowering the flow rate to the recommended brew time of 2:30~3:00 makes ordinary coffee flat and washed-out, dark in tone, and bitter.
- Increase the dose slightly to **13~15g**
- Reduce beverage weight by 10 ml to a **155 ml target** (pour 200g but cut it off partway)
- Brew time **1:30~1:40**
- Reason: the last 10 ml takes more than 10~20 seconds to extract, so cutting off partway avoids the balance collapse of extracting at too low a flow rate

## Espresso — Light-Roast Single Origin (Source 14)

| Item | Value |
|---|---|
| Pressure | **6bar** early → **4bar** late (4~6bar range) |
| Preinfusion | **5~10 s**, gentle pressure increase |
| Temperature | 5~10 days off roast **86°C** / 10 days~3 weeks **90°C** / after 3 weeks **93°C or higher** |
| Dose | fixed at **20g** |
| Basket | VST 20g + Kalita round filter 53mm underneath |
| Brew speed | mostly **4ml/s or higher** |
| Brew time | **around 15 s** including preinfusion |
| Brew ratio | **1:3** by default; if there is bitterness or unpleasantness, **reduce by 5g at a time**. Measured optimum generally **1:2.2** |
| Puck prep | grind into dosing cup → shaking → WDT tool → Force Tamper |

**Pressure thresholds** [Observed]: **above 6bar** the flavor disappears and it tastes like a dark roast. **below 4bar** there is almost no crema and the taste is sharp and uncomfortable to drink. Set the target at 6bar and **adjust grind size coarser** to fit within the range.

**For light roasts, adjust with beverage weight, not grind size** — because meeting the pressure condition limits the grind-size range.

## Espresso — Dark Roast, Maximizing Crema (Source 09)

| Item | Value |
|---|---|
| Basket | IMS 24.5M (small open area) |
| Dose | 17.5g + puck screen (minimizes headspace) |
| Grind size | as coarse as possible |
| Preinfusion | fast (maximum flow rate) |
| Temperature | 86°C |
| Profile | flat if some acidity is still alive / declining pressure late for dark or robusta blends with no acidity |
| Peak late flow rate | 1.7~1.8ml/s (robusta blend, declining-pressure profile) |

Extract plenty of crema but prefer **splitting the shot with a two-spout to halve the amount of crema**. Splitting the shot is recommended unconditionally for dark roasts, and likewise for light roasts when harsh flavor should be reduced.

**A puck screen encourages edge-biased extraction, so use it only together with maximum-flow-rate preinfusion.**

## Extractamundo Dos! (by Joe D., Source 12)

A Decent profile. Designed for **the MP burr family, which gives high extraction yield**.

| Segment | Setting |
|---|---|
| Preinfusion | 8bar target (later changed to a flow-rate target with a maximum of 8ml/s) — compress hard in a short time to secure resistance |
| Dynamic blooming | stop the pump on reaching **4.5bar** → only down to **2.2bar** (1~2 s) |
| Extraction | flow limiter **1ml/s**, detection range **3ml/s** → flow velocity increases slowly from 1→4ml/s |

**Setting the limiter low and the detection range wide** makes it act as a weak limiter across the whole run, so the change in flow velocity is gentle. The goal is to use a fast flow velocity of 3~4ml/s while blocking anything above that, reducing harsh flavor.

**The problem with 30-second blooming**: pressure drops excessively and the particle spacing that was tightly packed during preinfusion **widens again under back pressure**. Extraction yield rises but resistance becomes very low. This suits body-focused grinders (Nitsche Zero) and machines with high water debit (LM), but with the combination of **a uniform grinder + low water debit** the extraction yield is excessively high and the sensory notes get worse. [Hypothesis]

**In Korea the Decent's maximum flow rate is 6ml/s because of the mains frequency.**

## U90 (by the author, Source 06)

A profile that imitates filter brewing on the Decent. The name is a play on V60 — a U shape + 90 degrees.

**Design goals**: ① raise the early flow rate to bring out flavor but **cap it to remove the astringent sensation** ② not flat, but **a high flow rate that then gradually decreases**, preventing over-extraction of the flavor compounds that run out late.

**Configuration**: a low-resistance high-yield basket + **2 paper filters** (removes fat-soluble compounds) + **no blooming**.

**Three phases**: high flow rate for the first 30 seconds → gradual decrease from 30 seconds → hold the minimum flow rate at the end.

### Getting-started guide for Decent users (verbatim from source)

- Start with a grind size similar to ordinary filter coffee
- Brew ratio **15.5g : 210g**
- **A high-yield basket + two paper filters** are required
- **WDT recommended**; for tamping, just level with about the weight of the tamper
- **A puck screen is not recommended**
- Early flow rate in three phases: **1.2~2.2 → 1.2 → 0.9 ml/s**
- Judge flow rate by **scale-based brew flow rate**, not the machine's display
- In the profile editor, turn on **water-tank preheating at 30°C** or the late-stage temperature drops

### The author's iteration log

1. Initial U90 → severe astringency → judged the early flow rate too high
2. Peak early flow rate **at or below 4ml/s** → **astringency completely undetectable**. But greenness appeared and the first sip was uncomfortably hot
3. **Left every other variable alone and lowered only the temperature by 3°C** → the greenness disappeared
4. Judged that the bean's main flavor is "grain-syrup sweetness" and does not suit strong acidity → lowered flow rate to **3ml/s** → moderate acidity early, good sweetness late
5. To emphasize sweetness alone, suggests trying as low as **2ml/s**

**This log is itself a model case of the diagnostic procedure** — change one variable at a time, check the sensory notes, move on to the next variable.
