# Variable Dictionary

The extraction variables and their directions as organized by the author. Source 16 (a beginner's guide to the variables) is the backbone, with figures from other posts merged in.

**Premise**: every item is the single-variable effect "with all other variables held fixed". The sources contain almost no quantitative thresholds — use direction only, and when a number is needed, take the user's own previous brew as the reference point.

Tier notation: `fundamental` = changes the total flavor load (not a prescription target) / `adjustable` = prescribable. The basis is the two variable tiers in `model.md`.

> Throughout this document set, a **higher brew ratio** means **less water** — 1:15 is higher than 1:16.

---

## Outcome Indicators

| Variable | Raising it |
|---|---|
| **Concentration (TDS)** | Intensity and duration of taste and flavor increase. The berry · green · citrus families get stronger. **Negative impressions such as fermented · sourness · bitterness · astringency · harshness increase along with them** |
| **Extraction yield (EY)** | The tone of the acidity darkens. Sweetness increases, then **past a certain point turns bitter and burnt** (non-monotonic). Certain coffees get a floral finish. The chance of astringency also increases |

## Adjustable Tier

| Variable | Manipulation | Result |
|---|---|---|
| **Brew temperature** | Raise | Concentration and yield increase. **Early on**, gas release gives resistance↑ · speed↓; **later on** the reverse, speed↑ (sign reversal) |
| **Drinking temperature** | Raise | Flavor intensity and duration increase. **Negative flavors increase along with them** — if the first taste is bad, lowering it to move the note change along faster may be better |
| **Dose** | Increase | Concentration↑, yield↓ |
| **Beverage weight** | Increase | Concentration↓, yield↑ |
| **Grind size** | Finer | Concentration and yield increase quickly, resistance↑ brew time↑. **Finer than the limit** and particles clump (espresso) or fines gather in the lower bed (filter) and clog the filter, harming uniformity and giving a negative taste |
| **Brew time** | Increase | The immersion proportion increases → **body↑, clarity↓**. Even at the same concentration and yield, a longer time means a higher immersion proportion |
| **Pressure** | Raise | Extraction of water-insoluble compounds increases → body↑ clarity↓. The coffee bed becomes unstable, uniformity↓. In espresso, crema↑ |
| **Water level (filter)** | Raise | The weight of the water raises pressure↑ → body↑ clarity↓. **The bypass water weight increases, so concentration and yield↓** |
| **Brew speed** | Fast | clarity↑, body↓ |
| **Bypass** | More | Concentration and yield↓, chance of edge-biased extraction↑. **The higher the resistance in the lower bed, the more the bypass increases** (watch the direction of causation) |

## Fundamental Tier

| Variable | Manipulation | Total flavor load |
|---|---|---|
| **Roast level** | Lower | Increases |
| **Grinder** | Flat burr · unimodal | Increases |
| **Grinder** | Conical burr · hand grinder | Decreases (strong in texture) |
| **Dose** (brew ratio fixed) | Increase | Increases |
| **Degassing** | Time passing | Decreases |

The dose item is an observation whose cause the author could not explain — even when 15g:240g / 18g:288g / 20g:320g are matched to the same TDS, 20g has the strongest flavor. "Theoretically it seems it ought to hold, but when you actually do it, that is how it comes out." [Observed] (Source 02)

## Filter Brewing Only

| Variable | Manipulation | Result |
|---|---|---|
| **Dripper** | Conical | Flow velocity at the bottom is fast and it holds a high flow rate for long → **much flavor extraction**. Flow rate plunges late, clogging |
| **Dripper** | Flat-bottom | Small rise in flow velocity, holds a low flow rate without clogging → **sweetness-centered** |
| **Dripper** | Bypass | Conical has much, flat-bottom has little |
| **Dripper material** | — | The degree of heat absorption differs, affecting brew temperature |
| **Paper filter** | Fast draining | Brew speed↑ → body↓ clarity↑ |
| **Paper filter** | Thick | Catches more fines, chance of clogging↓ |
| **Paper filter** | Unbleached (brown) | Tends toward more uniform hole size than bleached white |
| **Rinsing** | — | **Almost meaningless in sensory terms.** Brewed together with coffee, it is hard for human senses to tell apart. Only for the purpose of seating the filter |
| **Water stream** | Thin | Impact on the bed↓, brew time↑, water level↓ |
| **Water stream** | Thick | Agitation effect, brew time↓, water level↑ |
| **Pour height** | Raise | Impact on the bed↑. **Past a certain height it scatters together with air and the impact actually gets smaller** (non-monotonic) |
| **Agitation** | Increase | Yield↑. At the same time **fines move downward, resistance↑ brew speed↓** |
| **Swirling** | — | Knocks off the particles stuck to the wall and levels the bed → consistency↑. But the fines that come off settle, **late resistance↑** |
| **Pre-wet** | Longer | Bed resistance↓ → brew speed↑. **The higher the temperature, the bigger the effect of the pre-wet** |
| **Pre-wet water weight** | More | The overall brew time slows |
| **Number of pours** | Fewer | Water level↑ → **flow rate↑ → flavor↑** |

**Definition of blooming** (the author's own): the phenomenon where, at the spot hollowed out by the water stream, gas from deep down gathers and erupts upward together with water and fines. **It pulls the fines from deep down up to the top of the bed.** In hand drip, where the water level never rises above the bed, blooming keeps happening and the fines do not go down, so the speed difference between the early and late halves is small and there is almost no clogging. (Source 16)

## Espresso Only

| Variable | Manipulation | Result |
|---|---|---|
| **Basket open-area ratio** | High | Resistance↓ brew speed↑ |
| **Basket hole distribution** | Center-concentrated | Center-biased extraction → **yield↓ · consistency↑ · resistance↑ · speed↓** (see the item below) |
| **Basket size** | Large | Bed height↑ → proportion of even extraction↑ → **yield↑** (applies only to baskets that adjust the holes with size, like VST and La Marzocco) |
| **Headspace** | Wide | Takes long to reach peak pressure → an effect similar to preinfusion → **favorable for light roasts** |
| **Headspace** | Narrow | Pressure rises sharply → **favorable for dark roasts (body, crema)** |
| **Preinfusion time** | Longer | Uniformity and consistency↑, **puck resistance↓ → late speed↑**, crema↓ → favorable for light roasts |
| **Preinfusion flow rate** | Higher | Forms a dense wall at the bottom of the puck → **resistance↑ · crema↑ · yield↓** (see `brewers/espresso.md`) |
| **Tamping** | Hard | Resistance↑ speed↓. **The faster the pressure is ramped the bigger the difference, and with a long preinfusion the influence becomes negligible** |
| **Tamping** | curved · nutation | Resistance↑ at the edge only → induces center-biased extraction |
| **Tamper diameter** | Smaller than the basket | Edge resistance↓. Negligible influence with a fast pressure ramp, **but with a long preinfusion the edge-biased extraction can grow** |
| **Rotary distributor** | Pulled out deep and rotated | Centrifugal force raises edge resistance↑ → induces center-biased extraction |
| **Tapping** | A lot | Fine particles go down to the bottom → **keep it to the minimum number of times** |

**Unless there is a special purpose, always tamp the same way.**

**The three purposes of distribution**: breaking up clumped coffee / evening out the grind-size distribution / keeping the bed height uniform.
For evening out the grind-size distribution, **the most effective thing is shaking and mixing with a dosing cup or shaker**. When, as with a single-dosing grinder, mostly fines come out at the end of grinding or there is left-right deviation at the chute, shaking is especially effective. (Source 16)

### Center-biased extraction

Several variables (center-concentrated basket · curved tamping · nutation · rotary distributor) induce it in common.

- Because it is uneven extraction, **yield↓**
- But the pressure spreads evenly in a circle and the structure stabilizes, so **consistency actually improves**
- The puck edge densifies and acts as a wall → the effect of shrinking the basket's inner diameter → **overall resistance↑ → speed↓ time↑**

Do not treat it as "uneven extraction = bad, no exceptions". (Source 16)

## Fines

**Fines quantity and fines distribution are separate variables.** Mix them and the author's writing looks contradictory.

- **Fines quantity** → contributes to flavor. In the author's experiment, removing the fines changed TDS by only 0.04% and the time by only 3 seconds, yet **the sensory notes differed by "a very large margin" — the tone darkened, the acidity dropped, and the bergamot aroma disappeared**. [Verified, n=1] (Source 15)
- **Fines distribution** → governs the flow. When they go down (tapping · agitation · swirling), resistance↑ · clogging↑; when they go up (blooming), resistance is stable.

The author's hypothesis: the conventional wisdom ("fines = body and sweetness, boulders = flavor") is inverted, and **flavor compounds, being low-molecular-weight and light, are extracted largely from the fines, while sweetness and body are determined not by grind size but by dose and concentration.** [Hypothesis] (Source 15)

The author marked the reliability as low themselves — "it is a single result and of course the reliability will be very low, but".

## Sign Reversal by Equipment Type

**The same "increase in resistance" produces the opposite result depending on the equipment.** (Source 15)

- **Constant-flow profile (Decent, etc.)**: resistance↑ → pressure↑ → actually **slightly faster**
- **Gravity-fed filter coffee**: resistance↑ → **slower**
